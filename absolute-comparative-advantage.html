Absolute Advantage vs. Comparative Advantage

← Back to Home

Absolute advantage and comparative advantage are key concepts in international trade that explain how countries benefit from specialization and trade.


Key Concepts:

  • Absolute Advantage: The ability of an individual, firm, or country to produce a good or service using fewer resources than others. A country has an absolute advantage when it can produce more output with the same inputs.
  • Comparative Advantage: The ability to produce a good or service at a lower opportunity cost than another producer. Even if a country lacks absolute advantage, it may still have comparative advantage in certain goods.
  • Specialization: Countries should specialize in producing goods where they have comparative advantage, allowing for greater overall output and efficiency in global trade.
  • Trade Benefits: Both countries benefit from trade when they specialize according to comparative advantage, leading to increased consumption possibilities for both trading partners.
