AD-AS Model

The Aggregate Demand-Aggregate Supply (AD-AS) Model is a macroeconomic tool used to analyze the overall level of prices and output in the economy. It shows the relationship between the total quantity of goods and services demanded and supplied at different price levels.


Key Concepts:

  • Aggregate Demand (AD): The total quantity of goods and services demanded by all economic agents (households, businesses, government, and foreign buyers) at various price levels. The AD curve slopes downward, showing an inverse relationship between price level and quantity demanded.
  • Aggregate Supply (AS): The total quantity of goods and services that producers are willing and able to supply at various price levels. The AS curve has different shapes depending on the time horizon: perfectly elastic in the short run, upward sloping in the intermediate run, and vertical in the long run.
  • Macroeconomic Equilibrium: The point where AD equals AS, determining the equilibrium price level and real GDP. Shifts in either AD or AS can result in new equilibrium points.
  • Recessionary Gap: A situation where the economy is operating at an output level below potential GDP, resulting in unemployment and unused resources.
  • Inflationary Gap: A situation where the economy is operating at an output level above potential GDP, leading to upward pressure on prices and inflation.
