Balance Sheets

← Back to Home

A Balance Sheet is a financial statement that shows the assets, liabilities, and equity of a bank or financial institution at a specific point in time. The central bank's balance sheet is particularly important in understanding monetary policy implementation.


Key Concepts:

  • Assets: What a bank owns or is owed, including reserves, loans made to customers, securities, and other investments. For the Federal Reserve, primary assets include government securities and loans to banks.
  • Liabilities: What a bank owes, including deposits from customers and borrowing from other sources. For the Federal Reserve, primary liabilities include currency in circulation and reserve balances held by banks.
  • Equity (Net Worth): The difference between assets and liabilities. It represents the owners' stake in the institution and provides a cushion against losses.
  • Reserve Requirements: The percentage of deposits that banks must hold as reserves and cannot lend out. Changes in reserve requirements affect the money supply.
  • Open Market Operations: The Federal Reserve's buying and selling of government securities to influence the money supply and interest rates. These operations directly affect the central bank's balance sheet.
  • Quantitative Easing: Large-scale purchases of financial assets by the central bank to increase the money supply when traditional monetary policy is ineffective.
