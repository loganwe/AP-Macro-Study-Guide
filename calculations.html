Calculations to Know

This section covers important macroeconomic calculations and formulas that you should know for the AP Macro exam. Mastering these calculations will help you solve quantitative problems effectively.


Key Calculations:

  • Gross Domestic Product (GDP): The total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a specific period. Can be calculated using expenditure approach (C + I + G + NX), income approach, or value-added approach.
  • Unemployment Rate: (Number of Unemployed / Labor Force) × 100. The percentage of the labor force that is actively seeking employment but unable to find work.
  • Inflation Rate: ((CPI Current Year - CPI Previous Year) / CPI Previous Year) × 100. Measures the percentage increase in the general price level of goods and services.
  • Real GDP: Nominal GDP adjusted for inflation using a price index (Base Year). Allows for comparison of GDP across different time periods.
  • Money Multiplier: 1 / Reserve Requirement. Shows how many times an initial change in the money supply translates into total money supply changes.
  • Multiplier Effect: 1 / (1 - MPC) or 1 / MPS. Shows the amplified effect of fiscal policy on total economic output.
  • Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC): The fraction of additional income that consumers spend on consumption. MPC = Change in Consumption / Change in Income.
  • Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS): The fraction of additional income that consumers save. MPS = 1 - MPC.
  • Real Interest Rate: Nominal Interest Rate - Inflation Rate. Adjusts the nominal interest rate for the effects of inflation.
