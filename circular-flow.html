Circular Flow Model

← Back to Home

The Circular Flow Model illustrates how money, resources, and goods move between households, businesses, and the government in an economy. It shows the interdependence of different economic sectors.


Key Concepts:

  • Households: Supply labor and factors of production to firms. In return, they receive wages and income. Households then use this income to purchase goods and services from businesses.
  • Businesses (Firms): Purchase factors of production from households and use them to produce goods and services. They sell these products back to households and the government.
  • Government: Collects taxes from households and businesses, and uses this revenue to purchase goods and services, provide public services, and transfer payments to households.
  • Money Flow: Represents the flow of payments and income throughout the economy. Money flows from businesses to households as wages, and from households to businesses as consumer spending.
  • Real Flow: Represents the actual movement of goods, services, and factors of production between different economic sectors.
