Demand & Supply

← Back to Home

Supply and Demand are fundamental economic principles that determine the prices and quantities of goods and services in a market economy. The interaction between these forces creates market equilibrium.


Key Concepts:

  • Demand: The quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to purchase at various price levels. Demand typically has an inverse relationship with price—as price increases, quantity demanded decreases.
  • Supply: The quantity of a good or service that producers are willing and able to offer at various price levels. Supply typically has a direct relationship with price—as price increases, quantity supplied increases.
  • Market Equilibrium: The point where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, resulting in a stable market price. At equilibrium, there is no tendency for the price to change.
  • Shortage: A situation where quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at a given price, typically resulting in upward pressure on prices.
  • Surplus: A situation where quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at a given price, typically resulting in downward pressure on prices.
  • Determinants of Demand: Factors that shift the demand curve include consumer preferences, income, prices of related goods, and expectations about future prices.
  • Determinants of Supply: Factors that shift the supply curve include input costs, technology, number of producers, and expectations about future prices.
