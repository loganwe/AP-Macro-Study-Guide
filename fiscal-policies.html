Fiscal Policies

← Back to Home

Fiscal Policy refers to the use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy. It is a key macroeconomic policy tool used to stabilize economic fluctuations and promote long-term economic growth.


Key Concepts:

  • Expansionary Fiscal Policy: Government increases spending or decreases taxes to stimulate economic growth during recessions. This increases aggregate demand, leading to higher output and employment.
  • Contractionary Fiscal Policy: Government decreases spending or increases taxes to reduce inflation during periods of excessive economic growth. This decreases aggregate demand, slowing economic expansion.
  • Multiplier Effect: The phenomenon where a change in government spending or taxes has a magnified effect on overall economic output. The multiplier shows how many times an initial change in spending translates into total economic activity.
  • Budget Deficit: A situation where government spending exceeds government revenue, resulting in borrowing to finance the gap. Deficits can stimulate the economy but may lead to long-term economic challenges.
  • Budget Surplus: A situation where government revenue exceeds spending, resulting in positive savings. Surpluses can slow economic growth but reduce government debt.
  • Crowding Out Effect: Government borrowing to finance deficits can drive up interest rates, reducing private investment and potentially offsetting some of the stimulus effects.
