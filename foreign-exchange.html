Foreign Exchange Market

The Foreign Exchange Market is where currencies are traded and exchange rates are determined. Understanding this market is crucial for analyzing international trade, capital flows, and the effects of monetary and fiscal policies on the global economy.


Key Concepts:

  • Exchange Rate: The price of one currency in terms of another currency. A higher exchange rate means the currency is stronger and appreciates in value.
  • Demand for Currency: Foreigners demand a country's currency to buy its exports, invest in its assets, or for other economic transactions. Higher returns or stronger economic fundamentals increase demand.
  • Supply of Currency: Domestic residents supply their currency when buying foreign goods, investing abroad, or engaging in other international transactions.
  • Exchange Rate Equilibrium: The exchange rate where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, determined by the balance of trade, capital flows, and interest rate differentials.
  • Appreciation: An increase in the value of a currency relative to other currencies, making exports more expensive and imports cheaper.
  • Depreciation: A decrease in the value of a currency relative to other currencies, making exports cheaper and imports more expensive.
  • Effect of Fiscal Expansion: Expansionary fiscal policy increases interest rates and attracts foreign investment, increasing demand for the currency and causing appreciation.
  • Effect of Monetary Expansion: Expansionary monetary policy lowers interest rates, reducing demand for the currency and causing depreciation.
