Loanable Funds Market

← Back to Home

The Loanable Funds Market illustrates the interaction between the supply of and demand for loanable funds, determining the equilibrium real interest rate. This market helps explain how fiscal policy and other factors affect investment and savings.


Key Concepts:

  • Supply of Loanable Funds: Sources of funds available for lending, including household savings, business savings, government surplus (if any), and foreign investment. Higher interest rates encourage greater savings and thus greater supply.
  • Demand for Loanable Funds: The amount of funds that borrowers (businesses and government) want to borrow at various interest rates. Typically, lower interest rates increase demand for loanable funds.
  • Equilibrium Interest Rate: The real interest rate at which quantity supplied equals quantity demanded for loanable funds. This rate coordinates saving and investment decisions.
  • Effect of Fiscal Expansion: Increased government spending or tax cuts increase demand for loanable funds, raising the equilibrium interest rate and crowding out private investment.
  • Effect of Increased Savings: When households or businesses increase their savings, the supply of loanable funds increases, lowering the equilibrium interest rate and encouraging more investment.
  • Foreign Capital Flows: Inflows of foreign investment increase the supply of loanable funds, while outflows reduce it, affecting domestic interest rates and investment.
