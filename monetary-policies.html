Monetary Policies

← Back to Home

Monetary Policy refers to the actions taken by the central bank (Federal Reserve in the US) to control the money supply and interest rates to achieve macroeconomic objectives. It is another key tool for managing economic fluctuations.


Key Concepts:

  • Expansionary Monetary Policy: The central bank increases the money supply and lowers interest rates to stimulate economic growth. This makes borrowing cheaper for businesses and consumers, encouraging spending and investment.
  • Contractionary Monetary Policy: The central bank decreases the money supply and raises interest rates to reduce inflation. This makes borrowing more expensive, discouraging spending and investment.
  • Discount Rate: The interest rate at which the central bank lends to commercial banks. Lowering the discount rate encourages borrowing and expands the money supply.
  • Reserve Requirements: The percentage of deposits that banks must hold as reserves. Lowering reserve requirements allows banks to lend more, increasing the money supply.
  • Open Market Operations: The primary tool used by the central bank, involving the buying and selling of government securities to directly control the money supply.
  • Federal Funds Rate: The interest rate at which commercial banks lend reserve balances to each other overnight. The central bank targets this rate through open market operations.
  • Transmission Mechanism: The process through which monetary policy affects the real economy, typically involving changes in interest rates leading to changes in investment and consumption.
