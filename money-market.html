Money Market

The Money Market illustrates the interaction between the supply of money and the demand for money, determining the equilibrium interest rate in the economy. It shows how monetary policy affects interest rates and the broader economy.


Key Concepts:

  • Money Supply (MS): The total amount of money circulating in the economy, controlled by the central bank. The money supply is typically shown as a vertical line on the money market diagram, indicating it is fixed in the short run.
  • Money Demand (MD): The amount of money that households and businesses want to hold at various interest rates. Money demand has an inverse relationship with interest rates—as rates rise, people demand less money.
  • Money Market Equilibrium: The point where money supply equals money demand, determining the equilibrium interest rate. This rate clears the market for money balances.
  • Effect of Increased Money Supply: An increase in the money supply shifts the MS curve right, lowering the equilibrium interest rate. This stimulates borrowing and investment.
  • Effect of Decreased Money Supply: A decrease in the money supply shifts the MS curve left, raising the equilibrium interest rate. This reduces borrowing and investment.
  • Liquidity Preference: The concept that people prefer having liquid money on hand rather than other assets, particularly when they face uncertainty about future expenses.
