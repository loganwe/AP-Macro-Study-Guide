Phillips Curve Model

← Back to Home

The Phillips Curve illustrates the relationship between unemployment and inflation in the economy. It shows the trade-off between these two macroeconomic variables, with significant implications for policymakers.


Key Concepts:

  • Original Phillips Curve: An inverse relationship between the rate of unemployment and the rate of wage inflation. When unemployment is low, inflation tends to be high, and vice versa.
  • Short-Run Phillips Curve: The downward-sloping relationship between unemployment and inflation in the short run. Policymakers can exploit this trade-off through expansionary or contractionary policies.
  • Long-Run Phillips Curve: A vertical line at the natural rate of unemployment, suggesting that in the long run, there is no permanent trade-off between unemployment and inflation.
  • Expectations-Augmented Phillips Curve: Incorporates inflation expectations, showing that the trade-off between unemployment and inflation depends on what people expect inflation to be.
  • Stagflation: A situation with both high inflation and high unemployment, which challenges the traditional Phillips Curve relationship and occurs during supply shocks.
  • Shifts in the Phillips Curve: Changes in inflation expectations, supply shocks, or credibility of monetary policy can shift the Phillips Curve, changing the unemployment-inflation trade-off.
