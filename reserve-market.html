Reserve Market (Ample Reserves)

← Back to Home

The Reserve Market refers to the market for bank reserves, where banks borrow and lend reserve balances. In the ample reserves framework, the Federal Reserve maintains a large supply of reserves to control interest rates through the standing facilities approach.


Key Concepts:

  • Reserve Balances: Electronic deposits held by banks at the Federal Reserve. These reserves are essential for banks to meet their reserve requirements and conduct interbank transactions.
  • Federal Funds Market: The market where banks lend reserve balances to each other overnight. The interest rate in this market is the federal funds rate, which the Fed targets.
  • Ample Reserves Framework: A monetary policy framework where the Federal Reserve maintains reserves well above the minimum required, allowing it to control the federal funds rate through standing facilities rather than open market operations.
  • Discount Window: A facility where banks can borrow directly from the Federal Reserve at the discount rate. This provides a floor for the federal funds rate.
  • Reverse Repurchase Agreement (Reverse Repo): A transaction where the Fed sells securities to banks with an agreement to buy them back later. This absorbs excess reserves and sets a ceiling for the federal funds rate.
  • Interest on Reserve Balances (IORB): The interest rate paid by the Fed on reserves held by banks. This influences the federal funds rate by affecting the opportunity cost of holding reserves.
